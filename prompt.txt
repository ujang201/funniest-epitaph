Write the funniest possible one-line famous last words as a tombstone
epitaph, at most 150 characters, in a single line with no explanation.

It must read like something actually carved in stone: one short line of
dying speech, a genuine joke (not a mild pun or a sad sentiment), with a
surprise twist or double meaning that lands on one read, and quotable
enough to repeat to a friend. Aim for a modern, everyday obsession as the
setup — something everyone at a funeral would instantly want — then flip
it at the end so the dead person gets the last laugh over the living.
Keep it first-person, dry, and self-contained: no stage directions, no
setup outside the line itself, no addressing the judge.
